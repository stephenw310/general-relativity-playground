Three new missions: Time, Motion, Waves.

Spacetime Curvature, Black Holes, and Gravitational Lensing show how mass shapes space and light. The landing page already reserves three more slots — these mocks fill them: what curvature does to time, how things move through it with no forces at all, and how changes in curvature travel. Each mock is a full mission screen in the existing console language, with design notes below the fold.

04 · TIMEtime-dilation

Time Dilation

A field of clock probes ticking at their own proper rates down a gravity well. Drag them deeper, set them orbiting, then run ten years of far time and compare how much each one aged.

Signature moment — the proper-time ledger: aging bars race apart, and Miller's planet turns one hour into seven years.

05 · MOTIONgeodesics

Geodesics

A slingshot launcher for probes and photons around a compact mass. Trails persist — rosettes, zoom-whirls, captures, escapes — while an effective-potential inset explains every turn.

Signature moment — Newton's closed ellipse ghosted under the precessing GR rosette: one glance shows exactly where Einstein disagrees.

Gravitational Waves

A binary sheds a rotating spiral of wave crests while a detector ring stretches and squeezes and a LIGO-style strain trace chirps toward merger — with the audio to match.

Signature moment — release the binary and hear the chirp climb 35→250 Hz into merger and ringdown. Spacetime, audibly ringing.

Why these three, in this order

They finish the classical GR arc

The live trio answers "how does mass shape space and light?" The new trio answers the remaining questions a curious visitor actually asks: what happens to time, why do orbits look like that, and can gravity itself move. Together the six cover a first course in general relativity — and clear the runway for the cosmology wing.

They're already promised

The mission-select page ships with slots 04–06 marked "in development" under the categories Time, Motion, and Waves, with accent colors reserved (teal, emerald, rose). These designs slot straight into the existing metadata in src/constants/simulations.ts — no landing-page rework needed.

Each has a distinct verb and a distinct wow

Place clocks and compare aging. Launch probes and collect orbits. Release a binary and listen to it die. No two missions share an interaction pattern, and each one climaxes in a moment tied to a real measurement: GPS clock corrections, Mercury's perihelion, GW150914.

Considered and parked

Strong candidates that lost on sequencing, not on merit.

Kerr frame-dragging Spinning black holes drag space around with them — but it's best built as an upgrade to Mission 02's ray tracer (spin slider, ergosphere), not a separate mission.
Cosmic expansion & redshift Opens the cosmology wing you have planned. Deserves its own visual language and a batch of siblings (CMB, distance ladder) rather than riding along here.
Relativistic flight · Terrell rotation Special relativity's "ride a photon" tour is spectacular but tonally separate; strongest once Mission 04 has introduced clocks and speed.
Twin-paradox trip planner A great companion feature for Mission 04 once its clock engine exists — plan a round trip, get your sibling's age gap — rather than a standalone scene.

From mock to build

  • Each mock reuses the live console layout — topbar, brief panel, control rail, stage — so promotion means a new route plus a stylesheet like src/app/styles/lensing.css, not a redesign.
  • The SVG scene in each mock is the storyboard for the real render: an R3F Canvas with GLSL where it earns it (wavefield, well surface) and plain instanced meshes where it doesn't (probes, trails).
  • State follows the lensing pattern: one zustand store per mission (time-dilation-store.ts, …) with selector hooks.
  • All physics named in the mocks is closed-form or cheap ODE integration — no new heavy machinery. Formulas are listed per mission in the notes below each mock.
  • Every mock ends with open questions — answer those first, then build.
