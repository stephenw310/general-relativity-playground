Design notes — mission 04

A field of clocks, and a ledger that makes them comparable

The pedagogical core is not any single clock — it is the comparison. Every probe visibly ticks at its own proper rate, and the ledger turns "time runs slower" into a number of years you can feel.

What the simulation is

  • Scene — a cross-section of a star's gravity well; the mid contour is the sheet probes sit on.
  • Actors — draggable clock probes whose faces pulse at their local dτ/dt, plus a far-away reference.
  • Payoff — run 10 years of far time and watch the aging bars race apart.

You control / you observe

  • Drag any probe to a new altitude; its tick rate responds instantly.
  • Morph the central body: Earth → white dwarf → neutron star → black hole.
  • Toggle hover vs circular orbit per probe to add the velocity term.
  • Presets anchor the effect in reality: GPS, ISS, Miller's planet.

Physics under the hood

  • Static observer: dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r)
  • Circular orbit: dτ/dt = √(1 − 3rs/2r) → 0.707 at the ISCO
  • Hover + speed: √(1 − rs/r − v²/c²)
  • GPS check: gravity +45.7 μs/day, speed −7.2 → net +38.5 μs/day
  • As r → rs the rate → 0 — the natural cliffhanger into Mission 02.

Open questions to iterate on

  • Log-scale mass slider (Earth → Sgr A*) or curated presets only?
  • 2D cross-section as mocked, or reuse Mission 01's 3D sheet for continuity?
  • Should each probe emit an audible tick? The desynchronizing rhythm could be the signature.
  • Is the redshift beam a default overlay or an unlockable detail?
