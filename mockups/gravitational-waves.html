Design notes — mission 06

Three synchronized views of one dying orbit

The wavefield shows what the binary radiates, the detector ring shows what a wave does when it arrives, and the strain trace ties both to the famous measured chirp — including as sound.

What the simulation is

  • Scene — a compact binary radiating a two-armed spiral of wave crests, rotating with the orbit.
  • Actors — the binary, a far detector ring of test masses, and a live strain chart.
  • Payoff — release the system and ride the full story: inspiral, chirp, merger flash, ringdown.

You control / you observe

  • Set the two masses and initial separation; the waveform reshapes live.
  • Release: separation and period shrink, frequency and amplitude climb.
  • Switch + and × polarization on the detector ring.
  • Audio on by request: the chirp sits right in hearing range.

Physics under the hood

  • Quadrupole radiation: f_GW = 2 f_orb, pattern rigid with the orbit
  • Inspiral from energy loss: dE/dt ∝ −(m₁m₂)²(m₁+m₂)/a⁵
  • Chirp rate set by the chirp mass ℳ = (m₁m₂)³ᐟ⁵/(m₁+m₂)¹ᐟ⁵
  • GW150914 preset: 36 + 29 → 62 M☉, with 3.0 M☉c² radiated away.

Open questions to iterate on

  • Is audio opt-in or the headline? "Hear spacetime ring" could open the mission.
  • Full inspiral in real time (0.2 s) needs a slow-motion factor — how slow?
  • Show the detector ring in-scene at distance, or keep it as an inset instrument?
  • Add LIGO's actual GW150914 data as a ghost trace behind the model?
